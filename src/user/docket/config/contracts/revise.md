# Charter
Revise the report rejected by the read-gate. Use that report as the base and
the tribunal's actual rationales as the work list. Verify the disputed claims
and return a complete, evidence-supported report. The gate owns approval.

Apply the fragments within this revision-only, read-only assignment.

# Not
Do not restart the investigation or repeat unaffected reproduction and bisection.
Reuse prior evidence while its inputs and conditions remain applicable. Reopen
only disputed work and claims affected by evidence uncovered during this revision.

Do not weaken claims merely to obtain approval. Correct false claims, withdraw
unsupported certifications, and state uncertainty where the evidence requires it.
Every rejecting rationale receives a supported answer or an explicit gap.

Do not change the checkout, shared repository metadata, or the affected system.
Use the archetype's isolated scratch workspace for permitted probes that write.
Fragment guidance about instrumentation or repairs does not authorize those
changes here. Use only the brief's authorized artifact and engine-recording paths.

# Method
Establish the issue and run, rejected gate instance and proposal, rejected report
artifact, and pinned target from the supplied context or the brief's explicitly
permitted retrieval route. Verify that they belong to the same rejection. An
issue-wide latest artifact is insufficient without that link. Missing or ambiguous
rejection context is an input gap; do not reconstruct it from recency or memory.

Read the rejected report and every seat's vote and rationale, including approving
seats' concerns. Preserve references to the proposal, seat, and each distinct
rejecting point so compound or overlapping rationales do not lose coverage.

Check each point against the report, governing requirements, and relevant evidence.
Inspect repository claims at the pinned target; use applicable retained runtime
artifacts or source documents for claims the repository cannot settle. Check
report omissions and presentation objections against the report and its contract.
A vote supplies an objection, not proof or authority to change requirements.

Use existing evidence first, then the smallest permitted check that resolves a
remaining question. Distinguish confirmed, contradicted, partly supported, and
unresolved points. For a mixed rationale, address each part. Fix confirmed errors
where they occur; answer contradicted points there with counter-evidence. Keep
unresolved points and their effect on the conclusion explicit.

When a check changes a premise, control, or evidence interpretation, reassess every
dependent claim and recommendation, including the opening conclusion. Preserve
unaffected findings, material conflicts, alternatives, coverage limits, and gaps.
Address relevant approving-seat concerns within the same boundary; they do not
authorize a broader investigation.

Preserve inherited citations, conditions, and provenance. OBSERVED, REPRODUCED,
and INFERRED describe diagnostic provenance under `truth-first`, not confidence.
Keep research provenance separate. Cite new or corrected claims under
`evidence-rules`; identify fresh checks without implying inherited work was rerun.

# Emit
`investigation` (markdown): the complete corrected report, conclusion first,
evidence underneath, then the recommendation. The re-run gate reads only this
artifact. Include the decisive evidence content and conditions as well as citations;
do not require access to the prior report or votes to understand a load-bearing claim.

Retain the supported findings, causal and action confidence, material conflicts,
surviving alternatives, coverage, exclusions, discoveries, and outstanding gaps.
Amend or withdraw invalidated claims explicitly. Identify the report being revised
and the target assessed.

End with a short "Revision" section. For every rejecting point, include its vote
reference, faithful summary, disposition, decisive evidence or remaining gap, and
the resulting report change or reason the claim stands. List approving-seat
concerns addressed there too. This section supplements corrections in the body.

# Stuck
Use the brief's `gap` protocol when required rejection context or evidence is
unavailable, a rationale remains unresolved, or the next necessary action requires
new investigation, instrumentation, repairs, or authority beyond this revision.
Name the specific point, what is established, what remains unknown, and how it
limits the report. Identify the cheapest safe next probe or required decision,
its discriminating outcomes, and the access or authority it needs. State when a
probe can inform only a future occurrence.

Finish independent corrections and preserve supported findings in `investigation`
alongside the gap. Follow the brief's recording protocol without inventing a
completion status. Do not claim approval, start another round, or reset the loop;
the workflow owns exhaustion and escalation to the operator.
